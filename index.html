01 · Context

Supernova is winning. That's why this matters now.

Supernova has built one of India's biggest consumer AI products: a voice tutor that teaches English in the learner's own language. Growth is real, paid subscribers tripled after the move to Sarvam's voice stack, and a new $15–20M round is reportedly in progress.

The unhappy users are a minority. Only 2.5% of 324K ratings are 1★. But that minority is concentrated at the moment revenue is actually earned: the first debit. It is also very public, with the words "fraud" and "scam" on the store page that every ad click lands on.

The APM job description asks for someone to "run hundreds of A/B tests across prompts, models, product flows". This case study is one of those flows, the one that touches revenue first.

03 · Diagnosis

Three problems, one broken first week

These aren't three separate bugs. They happen one after another to the same learner, and each one makes the next feel worse.

Day 0

Sees an ad

"₹9", "free classes", "a robot that talks to you".

Day 0

Paywall before value

Paywall after lesson 1; pop-up close button on a delay.

Problem 2
Day 0

Pays ₹9 "to test"

Unknowingly approves a UPI AutoPay mandate up to ₹299–₹1,799.

Problem 1
Day 3

₹299 disappears

No reminder they notice. No obvious cancel button in the app.

Problem 1
Day 3+

The app breaks

"Analyzing audio" hangs, chats vanish, Premium doesn't unlock.

Problem 3
Day 4

1★ + refund + mandate revoked

Gets a canned reply. Revenue, rating and trust are all lost.

The root cause. Supernova replies to almost every one of these reviews saying the ₹299 renewal is "shown at checkout and in your UPI app." That's true, and it's exactly the problem: a disclosure the user doesn't absorb is not an expectation. Price, value and control reach the user in the wrong order: price before value, the debit before a reminder, and payment before reliability.

Why #1 leads

It's the only theme that is large, still rising and almost entirely 1★, and it's tied directly to revenue already earned and to regulatory risk. India's CCPA dark-pattern guidelines name "subscription trap" and "drip pricing", and RBI's 2026 e-mandate rules require a pre-debit notice. All of this matters just as investors start due diligence.

Why fix all three in one flow

Fix only the debit and users still hit a paywall before they've seen any value. Fix the paywall and the debit, and paying users still churn when the app hangs. All three are steps of one funnel, and the metric that matters, paid retention into month 2, only moves if every step holds.

05 · Strategy

Three principles for the first week

PRINCIPLE 1

Value before price

The learner speaks with Nova and gets a real correction before seeing any price. Premium is offered once, at the moment of value, with a "Not now" that works instantly.

PRINCIPLE 2

No surprise money

The price, date and amount are shown as a timeline, confirmed actively, and repeated in Supernova's own reminder 24 hours before the debit. Cancel, pause or get a refund in one tap.

PRINCIPLE 3

Never block a learner

Feedback appears instantly and the detailed analysis follows in the background. History is saved on the phone first. Payment unlocks itself, with a fallback and a ticket ID.

What I'm deliberately not changing

  • The ₹9 trial and UPI AutoPay. They're the growth engine; the problem is the surprise, not the model.
  • Prices. ₹299/month stays. A quarterly plan is added as a save offer.
  • Curriculum and speech-model accuracy. Real problems (5% each), but next quarter's work.

The trade-off I'm betting on

A visible reminder and one-tap cancel will lower the day-3 conversion rate somewhat. I'm betting it raises paid retention into month 2 per trial start, which is revenue that survives refunds, disputes and revoked mandates, while removing regulatory risk. The A/B test settles it. If trial starts fall more than 5%, we roll back.

A/B test

50/50 split of new Android installs from paid ads, run for 4 weeks. That's enough to see every user reach their first debit and the start of month 2. Variant: the full flow. Control: today's flow. Analysis is per trial start, so pushing more trials can't game the result.

Rough sizing

ARR of about $15–17M is roughly ₹125–140 Cr a year, or about ₹11 Cr a month. Every 1% of monthly revenue kept from refunds, disputes and early mandate cancellation is worth about ₹11L a month (about ₹1.3 Cr a year). That excludes the lower support cost, the store rating, and paid-acquisition conversion on a store page that currently says "scam". I can't see Supernova's actual refund or cancellation rates; the test measures them.
